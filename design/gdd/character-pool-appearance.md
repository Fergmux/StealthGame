## Summary

Character Pool & Appearance defines the 6 to 8 base models (at most 24 appearances) that both players and NPCs wear, and the one rule that hands them out: a balanced, shuffled, team-blind appearance deck (confirmed by the user 2026-10-25) dealt once per round, then conserved so that kills, respawns, disconnects and rejoins never change the mix of looks. Players never operate it; they feel a believable, varied crowd in which a look narrows the search but never answers it. It exists because Pillar 1 must hold statistically as well as visually: with an unbalanced duplicate rule, how many copies of a look existed told an opponent whether players wore it.

> **Quick reference** — Layer: `Core` · Priority: `MVP` · Key deps: `Tuning Data & Config` (hard); `Participant State & Roster`, `Round & Match Framework` (caller), `Session & Travel` (soft, provisional)

## Overview

Character Pool & Appearance (CPA) defines every look a character in Smoking Mirror can have and the one rule that hands those looks out. The **pool** is a cooked data table of 6 to 8 base models with up to 3 variants each (default, garment-swatch swap, accessory combination), never more than **24 distinct appearances** (MVP floor: 6 models x 2 variants = 12, built from mannequin variants first), each addressed by a one-byte `AppearanceId`. The **allocator**, `AllocateAppearances(Players, NpcCount)`, is a pure server-side function that Round calls once per round in `Preparing`. It builds a single **appearance deck** (at most 12 appearances in play, at least 4 copies of each, per-appearance counts differing by at most 1), shuffles it with a server-private random stream, and deals it to the whole crowd of ten players and about fifty NPCs without ever being told who is on which team or which pawn is a player. After the deal, the deck is **conserved**: an NPC killed is replaced by a new NPC wearing the card the victim vacated, and a participant who loses Active status adopts a **different**, unobserved crowd pawn whose look is drawn uniformly from those pawns, so the look a killer saw never follows the victim; the mix of looks in the city never drifts and never changes in a way that correlates with player-ness.

The system exists to make Pillar 1 true at the level of statistics, not just art. The art bible makes the models individually uniform (`design/art/art-bible.md` 1.3); CPA makes the *crowd* uniform: no look is a player look, no look count is a hint, no look changes when a player is hit, hides, disconnects or respawns. An earlier rule ("at least three NPC duplicates for each player appearance") is **retired** because the appearances that players held then had systematically more copies than the filler appearances, so counting copies leaked who the players were. The deck is the **only** allocation rule.

CPA is mostly invisible by design. Its player-facing surface is the sight of a believable, varied crowd; its design surface is a short set of contracts the other systems must honour: Round (when the deal happens), Crowd & NPC Behaviour (spawning and replacing by card), Participant State & Roster (the server-only participant-to-card map lives beside it, never on `PlayerState`), the Information Visibility Policy (`AppearanceId`, audience `All`, never linked to a participant), Shared Character & Locomotion (the one pawn property and its application), and Tuning Data & Config (the deck parameters). Implementation choices (class names, replication mechanism) are noted as "-> ADR" candidates; the behaviour is what this document fixes. Anything depending on the Meshy retarget spike, the 50-character performance spike or net-emulation results is marked **provisional pending spike**.

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: indirect (the player never touches this system; they live inside its result). Shaped by creative-director consult: framing B ("That stranger could be anyone") with framing A's line as the mirror.*

**The look narrows; the behaviour decides.** You follow an ochre cloak behind a cart and three ochre cloaks come out the other side. What you saw stays true: nobody changes their face, their cloth, or their count when you are not looking. The city is a believable crowd, varied at a glance, and the repeats only show when you hunt one particular person. That is the moment the system is for: appearance stops being the answer, and you have to watch *how* someone moves, pauses and turns. That is the one skill the game sells (Core Fantasy: "invisible because you look like everyone, lethal because you can tell who doesn't belong").

The mirror: you are shelter and prey in the same coat. Nobody's look is safe and nobody's look is guilty, so the stranger in your colour could be your rival, your teammate or a baker's errand-runner, and the hunter behind you faces the same lottery. When a read goes wrong, the outfit did not lie; your judgement did (Pillar 3 made fair).

**What the section promises the rest of the design** (tests, not slogans): (1) at a glance the crowd reads as many different people (variety is what the scene shows); (2) sameness is only something a hunter runs into, once tracking one specific person (sameness is what the hunt shows); (3) neither ever tells a player who another player is. **Language to use**: "worn by the city, not chosen by you", "the look narrows, the behaviour decides", "what you saw stays true". **Language to avoid** in any player-facing text: "clones", "identical crowd", "random skins", "disguise" (a tool you switch on), "unique look", "everyone has a twin" (a false promise about counts; a rare look would then read as "NPC"), and the mechanism words "balanced deck", "allocator", "shuffle" (they describe how, not how it feels).

Pillar link: Pillar 1 ("an enemy player cannot be told apart from an NPC by model, animation, audio, UI, or network presentation") and Pillar 2 (a read must come from chosen behaviour, never from a look). The first-scope anti-pillar "not a progression or cosmetics grind" is respected: there is no appearance choice, unlock or shop in MVP, and the future-tier guard (Core Rule CP-17) is written so a later cosmetic cannot become a tell.

## Detailed Design

### Core Rules

Terms: **appearance** = one (model, variant) row of the pool; **card** = one dealt copy of an appearance; **deck** = all cards of one round (size `D`); **bound** = a card held by a participant's pawn; **NPC card** = a card held by an unbound pawn. Only the server knows which cards are bound (`ServerOnly`).

**Pool**

- **CP-1 One pool table.** The pool is one cooked data asset (`UCharacterPoolData`-style, -> ADR) of appearance rows. Row fields: `AppearanceId` (uint8, stable authored key, 0 to 23, **not** derived from model, variant or pool size, so adding a model never renumbers the others), `Model` (skeletal mesh asset), `VariantKind` (`Default | GarmentSwap | Accessory`), `GarmentSwatchIndex` (0 to 7), `TrimSwatchIndex` (0 to 7), and a reference to a **static** `MI_Char_<Model>_<Variant>` material instance. There is no runtime material instance per pawn, so no parameter can be set per pawn (art bible 8.x.6 "no keyed branches"). Value `255` is the `Unassigned` sentinel and is never a live id.
- **CP-2 Size limits.** 6 to 8 base models in a shipping pool, each with 1 to 3 variants, **at most 24 appearances** (art bible 1.3.1). MVP floor: 6 models x 2 variants (`Default`, `GarmentSwap`) = 12 appearances. A pool below 6 models or 12 appearances may run in development and test builds (a warning is logged and `PoolBelowMvpFloor` is written to the Round Event Log header) but **fails the pool gate** (CP-15) and must not ship.
- **CP-3 Swatch assignment is a rule, not a choice** (art bible 4.3). For model `k` (1 to N, N = number of models) the active swatches are S1 to SN; indices are 1-based modulo N and the material parameter is swatch minus 1: `Default` garment S(k); `GarmentSwap` garment S(k+1); trim S(k+3) in **every** variant; `Accessory` variant garment S(k), trim S(k+3) (**assumption**: art bible 4.3 is silent on the accessory variant's garment; S(k) is chosen because it keeps the variant's mean garment L* unchanged). A pool validator (editor-time, CI) recomputes the indices from this rule, fails on any hand-edited value, and checks that every swatch occurs on exactly 3 distinct models across the garment-default, swap and trim roles.
- **CP-4 Accessory variants are separate mesh assets** (decision from the art-director consult), not a material mask: the pool master material is opaque with no masked opacity and no world-position offset, and accessories are merged geometry in one material slot (art bible 1.3.6, 8.x.6). Each accessory variant therefore carries its own LODs and goes through T1 to T4 individually. The `AccessorySetMask` parameter listed in art bible 8.x.6 is not used by this system (art bible change request in Cross-References). For MVP, variant 2 is `GarmentSwap` only (material-instance-only, no new geometry).
- **CP-5 Pool tiers.** Tier A (**MVP first**): six mannequin-derived models. Tier B: Meshy-generated full models normalised to the Manny skeleton (art bible 8.7). Tier C (**fallback**): one shared canonical body (Body_Base) with Meshy-generated heads, hair and garments, invoked if 3 of the first 6 Meshy bodies fail the Body_Base conform test (art bible 1.3.1, 8.7). The allocator, `AppearanceId`, replication and every rule below are **identical across tiers**; only the pool table's content changes. Tier A honesty: stock mannequins have no faces and Manny and Quinn differ in build, so Quinn is **not** counted as a model difference; Tier A "models" are recombinations of hair, headwear and garment-cut geometry on the Manny body, each baked into one merged single-slot mesh (the 1.3.1 shared-body kit, used earlier than planned). All of Tier A is **provisional pending the Meshy retarget spike and a Manny/Quinn measurement**.

**Allocation**

- **CP-6 Allocator contract.** `AllocateAppearances(Players, NpcCount)` is called by Round exactly once per round, in `Preparing`, after `OnTuningReady` and before any pawn is spawned. `Players` is an unordered set of opaque `ParticipantId` values (**no team, role, join-order or state field exists in its type**; team-blindness is enforced by the signature). `NpcCount` is the frozen TDC value. The function is pure given its inputs, the pool, the frozen deck parameters and a random stream. It returns an `AppearanceDeal`: (a) `ParticipantToAppearance` (**ServerOnly**), (b) `NpcAppearances`, a list of length `NpcCount` in dealt order, (c) the active set, counts and extras for logging, (d) `DealDigest`, a hash of the random seed (not the seed). It never reads team, role, objective, position or the layout.
- **CP-7 Deck construction** (F-1 to F-4). `D = |Players| + NpcCount`. `K` active appearances are chosen by the **model-stratified draw** (F-4); copies per appearance are `q = floor(D/K)`, with `r = D mod K` uniformly chosen appearances holding one extra. Every active appearance therefore has at least `DeckCopiesFloorCount` (4) copies and counts differ by at most 1.
- **CP-8 Shuffle and deal.** The deck is shuffled with an unbiased Fisher-Yates using rejection-sampled integers (no modulo bias). Participants are taken in canonical `ParticipantId` order and take the first `|Players|` cards; the remaining `NpcCount` cards are the NPC cards in the same order. Which participant takes which position is irrelevant to the result: the shuffled deck is exchangeable (F-5). No later step may re-sort cards by appearance.
- **CP-9 The random stream is server-private** (aligned with Session & Travel rule 14 and F5; revised in design review 2026-10-24). The allocator's root seed is `GetServerSubSeed(AppearanceDeal, 0)`, Session's keyed hash of the never-replicated `ServerSecret`; it is **never** the public layout `Seed`, nor anything computed from the round id, participant ids or tick count outside that keyed hash (round id is safe only as an input to the keyed hash). From the root, CPA derives **three domain-separated streams** by keyed hash with a stream tag: `Deal` (subset draw, extras set, shuffle), `Replace` (`DrawReplacement`) and `Take` (reserved: since reconciliation 2026-10-24 `TakeNpcCard` makes no draw of its own, Penalty CR-6), so the observable outputs of the last two can never be used to rewind the deal. Each stream is a fixed-algorithm, keyed counter-mode generator (a keyed-hash DRBG; a plain invertible PRNG such as PCG32 is not acceptable for the `Replace` and `Take` streams; the primitive is SipHash-2-4 in counter mode, `FSecretStream`, ADR-0006 §5) so results are reproducible across engine versions. Only `DealDigest` (a hash of the root seed, never the seed) is logged. Tests inject the entropy through an `IEntropySource` parameter of the allocator (or Session's non-shipping `ServerSecret` override); the injection hook is compiled out of Shipping.
- **CP-10 One spawn list; appearance before first replication; possession changes nothing others see.** Round builds **one spawn list of all `D` pawns** (Round P6), spawns them in one frame in a server-secret shuffled order (`GetServerSubSeed(SpawnOrder, ...)`) independent of player-ness, all of the **one exact pawn class** (same archetype path, same replicated subobject list); `AppearanceId` is set on every pawn by deferred spawn before it first replicates; and all `D` pawns are placed by the same emergence rule (Level Socket Contract; placement is independent of appearance and of player-ness). Player entries additionally get `BindPawn` and are possessed by a PlayerController, NPC entries by the Crowd AI controller; **possession must not change any replicated value observable by a non-owning client**: the pawn's `PlayerState` reference must not replicate to non-owners (stock `APawn` replicates it to everyone, so it is suppressed or made owner-only, -> ADR verify on UE 5.8 legacy replication and Iris), `Owner` and `Controller` resolve identically for NPC and player pawns on non-owners, `RemoteViewPitch` (or its Iris equivalent) has the same source and smoothing for both (Look path, Shared Character), and neither class uses net dormancy differently. The server-only participant-to-card map is the only record of binding. Crowd adoption (CP-12, CP-13) changes only the controller and binding of an existing pawn, so these rules are what keep it invisible on the wire.
- **CP-11 Immutable per pawn, conserved per round.** `AppearanceId` is written once per pawn. The deck is **conserved**: per-appearance card counts (`c_a`) are fixed at the deal for the whole round; no operation changes `D` or any `c_a`. The only operations that move cards are CP-12 and CP-13, and they move a card between states without creating or destroying one. Round's **own-card respawn** (Round R17.9: an `Active` participant's pawn lost without a resolved kill, for example KillZ) moves no card at all: the card stays `BoundLive`, and the one new pawn is spawned with the participant's current `AppearanceId` through Crowd's replacement path (`RequestEmergence(Kind = PawnRecovery)`), so `D` and every `c_a` are unchanged (reconciliation 2026-10-24, Round CR 15).
- **CP-12 Replacement draw and look re-roll** (F-6). (a) When an NPC is killed or removed, its card becomes `NpcVacant`. When Crowd spawns a replacement NPC it asks the allocator for `DrawReplacement()` **in the same server tick as the spawn** (so no card is ever `NpcLive` without a pawn), which returns a card chosen **uniformly at random from the currently vacant NPC cards** (stream `Replace`) and marks it `NpcLive`. Crowd's backlog policy (which vacancy's *timer* fires first) governs timing only; the look is always the uniform draw over all vacant cards. With a single vacancy this returns the victim's own appearance. A bound card is never in the vacant set, so replacement draws never touch player cards and never depend on them. (b) **A participant's look never survives its loss of Active status or its death as an informant** (fix from the CD-GDD-ALIGN gate). The kill confirmation tells a killer, and a reversal tells a defender, that a specific pawn was a participant; if that participant's next pawn wore the same look, the killer's team would hold a lasting handle on "the look with an enemy informant in it", an identification aid. Therefore: when a participant's pawn dies (assassinated, reversed against, informant killed) its card becomes `NpcVacant` exactly like a killed NPC's, and a same-look NPC re-emerges through `DrawReplacement`; when the pawn survives losing Active status (strike removal) it is released to the crowd as an NPC with its card (`NpcLive`, no visible change, as in CP-13b). The participant's informant pawn is always a **different pawn**, obtained by **crowd adoption only** (Participant State & Roster R9; revised in design review 2026-10-24): NPC-Kill Penalty & Informants obtains the pawn from Level Socket Contract's `PickRespawnPawn(Team, RequestSeq, ExcludePawn)` (LSC R5b), which makes the **one** uniform random pick among existing `Unbound` NPC pawns that no opposing viewer can currently see, and `TakeNpcCard(ParticipantId, Pawn)` moves **that pawn's** card `NpcLive -> BoundLive` with no second draw (reconciliation 2026-10-24, Penalty CR-6: one random draw only), in the same queue slot as Shared Character & Locomotion's `TakeOverCrowdPawn` (SCL R2a) and **only after it returned `Applied`** (the takeover binds the pawn first; no card move for a failed takeover; ADR-0010 §3.2, ADR-0019 §4); the possession follows at the next frame start. No actor is destroyed or created and `AppearanceId` is never rewritten (CP-11). There is **no** retire-and-spawn path and no fresh mid-round spawn of a participant pawn (a destroy/create pair is readable on the wire, and a pawn appearing at the team's own spawn is a location tell). The candidate set must be independent of appearance; any location restriction on it (for example near the team's zone) is NPC-Kill Penalty & Informants' decision and Pillar-1 review. **Ordering for strike removal** (reconciliation 2026-10-24, Penalty CR-6): the exile body is released to the crowd **at the exile** (`ReleaseToCrowd`, card `BoundLive -> NpcLive`); the participant adopts a different pawn at `T_due` (Penalty PEN-R9), and the released body is passed to `PickRespawnPawn` as `ExcludePawn`, so it can never be the pawn it adopts. **Several adoptions in one tick** are picked without replacement in Round's total order; a request with no candidate waits (CP-13c retry). `D` and all `c_a` are unchanged; the observable sequence for a participant victim matches an NPC victim's (the victim falls; one same-look pawn re-emerges out of sight after a delay drawn from the same distribution; the adoption itself shows nothing). **What the killer learns about the new look:** the informant's look is distributed like the looks of the unobserved NPC pawns at bind time, so for every pawn other than the forced-NPC replacement the chance of being the new informant is the same whatever its look ("per-pawn uniform except the forced-NPC replacement"). The same forced-NPC replacement follows every NPC kill, so the likelihood ratio between "my victim was a participant" and "my victim was an NPC" is 1 for every pawn (systems-designer review; assumes the observability of a pawn is independent of its look). At look level the same-look probability dips (about 3/49 against 4/49 for a 4-copy look at default size) only because the replacement pawn is certainly an NPC; that dip is not information about any pawn.
- **CP-13 Leave, rejoin, informant re-pawn.** (a) **Disconnect** (including a participant still `Disconnected` at Round P6): the participant has a bound pawn like everyone else, driven by Crowd's NPC controller **at once** (a frozen or missing pawn would be a tell; Roster R11, Round P6), and its card stays `BoundLive` until `DisconnectGraceSec` (Round-owned, default 30 s) expires; a rejoin inside the grace period re-possesses the same pawn and keeps the same card. (b) **Grace expired or permanent leave:** Round moves the participant to `Informant` (Reason `Disconnect`) and then `ReleaseToCrowd`; the card is **released to the NPC side** (`NpcLive`; the pawn stays as an NPC with the same `AppearanceId`; no visible change). If the pawn no longer exists the card is `NpcVacant` and is refilled by CP-12. `D` and all `c_a` are unchanged. (c) **No late join** (revised in design review 2026-10-24): Session & Travel admits only rejoins in-round (`RejoinOnly`, rules 8 and 20) and TDC CR-8 forbids joins after `Frozen`, so no new participant ever takes a card mid-round. `TakeNpcCard` is used only to re-pawn an **informant** by crowd adoption (CP-12b): after its pawn died, and for a returning informant "awaiting pawn" that Round re-admits after grace expiry (Round R18). `D` and all `c_a` are unchanged and the effective NPC count falls by 1; Round checks TDC invariant I-2 (`NpcCount >= 2 * TeamSize`, on the effective count) before re-admitting. If no candidate exists, NPC-Kill Penalty & Informants waits and re-picks per LSC R5b.5; `LateJoinTakeoverTimeoutSec` and its view-only fallback do not apply to informants (reconciliation 2026-10-24, Penalty CR-7). (d) **Host loss (listen server):** the session ends (no migration in MVP); the next session re-deals.
- **CP-14 Every round re-deals.** `Reset` discards the deck, the active set and the map. Nothing about a participant's previous appearance carries into the next round (a persistent look would let opponents remember who is who). Match-scoped persistence: none.
- **CP-15 Pool admission gates** (art bible 1.3.7). A model enters the pool only when it passes T1 (numeric audit) and T2 to T4 (line-up at 15 m, greyscale silhouette, motion and skin), with captures retained in `production/qa/evidence/`; T5 (50-character crowd, 60 fps, animation at most 4 ms) re-runs whenever the pool, LOD or pool material changes. **No waivers.** CPA adds three gates: **G-VAR** (minimum variety: at 8 m in idle, at least 6 testers tell any two active models apart in at least 75 percent of paired trials, so a pool that passes T1 to T5 only by being near-identical cannot ship; thresholds **provisional**), **G-TWIN** (a capture of 4 copies of one appearance within 6 m, at 15 m and at close range, reads as an ordinary crowd with no per-instance tint, scale or dirt), and **G-DECK** (the statistical test of F-8). Any change in pool membership re-runs T1 items 10 to 12 and T2 for all models (swatch indices depend on N) and re-validates CP-3. The T2 and T3 pass thresholds (25 percent against a 12.5 percent chance level at 8 models) must be re-stated against the actual chance level (1/N models, or 1/appearances) when the pool size differs (art bible change request).
- **CP-16 One look for everyone, always.** The same `AppearanceId` yields the same mesh, material instance, LOD selection by screen size, skin-cache setting, physics asset, animation fidelity, shadow and reflection behaviour for every viewer and every pawn class (player, NPC, informant, concealed, alive, dead). No client-side or per-viewer appearance exists: no local tint, outline, highlight, scale, extra component, material parameter or audio selection keyed by the viewer, by "is local", by team or by `AppearanceId`. The owner's hidden first-person body is a local render flag (owner-no-see) on the owner's client and changes nothing others see; the teammate marker is UI, not appearance. The assassination prompt appears identically for any in-range pawn whatever its look (art bible 5.2). No two copies of an appearance differ (no per-instance random tint, scale, dirt or wetness).
- **CP-17 Future-tier guard.** There is no appearance choice, unlock or cosmetic in MVP. Any later cosmetic must enter the pool as a **new appearance in the deck at equal weight for everyone in the match** (and count toward the 24 cap), or be cut. A cosmetic equipped by one participant, owned by one participant, or granted by progression, rank, purchase or team must not change the appearance of any in-world pawn. No accessibility feature may give directional or player-only information beyond the default presentation, nor change another pawn's appearance (a colour-vision aid is a uniform full-screen transform on the viewing client only, never per pawn).
- **CP-18 Residency and version.** All pool entries (meshes, textures, material instances) are hard-referenced and resident on every client before the round can leave `Preparing`, and a pawn's first draw never triggers an asset load, so no appearance is slower to appear than another (a pop is a tell); the pawn draws nothing until `AppearanceId` has resolved from resident assets (no default or placeholder look, not even for a frame). Residency is not first-draw readiness: during the loading screen each client runs a hidden **warm-up of every pool entry** (all `A` appearances, never only the active `K`, or the warm-up itself would reveal the subset) so texture mips, skeletal-mesh LODs, skin cache and pipeline states are ready before the reveal (-> ADR verify texture streaming and PSO precache on 5.8). **No acknowledgement of CPA's own** (revised in design review 2026-10-24): the client computes `PoolHash` (hash of the pool table and its asset package versions) locally and it rides as one field in Session & Travel's **single round acknowledgement** (rule 11.6, beside `ClientHash`), which the client sends only once residency and warm-up are complete. A mismatch follows Session's resend-once then `ParamsMismatch` path; the only deadline is Session's `TravelLoadTimeoutSec` from travel start (rule 7); rejoiners are covered by the same acknowledgement. CPA adds no RPC, timer, kick rule or Reason code.
- **CP-19 Subset is per match and shared.** The active set (F-4) is chosen once per round, is the same for both teams, and is fixed for the round. It must include every base model at least once when `K >= M`, and at least `SwatchFloor = min(5, K, M)` distinct garment swatches (F-4).

### States and Transitions

**Card states** (server-only; a card is in exactly one state at any time):

| State | Meaning | Visible to clients? |
| ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `InDeck` | Exists only inside `AllocateAppearances`, between construction and deal | No |
| `BoundLive` | Held by a participant's living pawn (Active or Informant) | The pawn's `AppearanceId` only (audience `All`); the binding is `ServerOnly` |
| `NpcLive` | Held by a living unbound pawn | The pawn's `AppearanceId` only |
| `NpcVacant` | Its NPC was killed or removed and no replacement has been drawn yet | No |
| `Discarded` | Round `Reset`; all state dropped | -- |

| From | To | Trigger | Owner of the trigger |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `InDeck` | `BoundLive` / `NpcLive` | Deal (CP-8) | Round calls the allocator |
| `NpcLive` | `NpcVacant` | NPC killed (`OnPawnKilled`, `VictimKind = Npc`) or destroyed by a non-kill | Crowd / Assassination |
| `NpcVacant` | `NpcLive` | `DrawReplacement()` in the tick the replacement pawn spawns (CP-12) | Crowd |
| `BoundLive` | `NpcVacant` (pawn dies) or `NpcLive` (pawn survives, released to the crowd at the exile, before the adoption) | Player eliminated (assassinated, reversed against, struck out); the participant then adopts a **different** pawn via `TakeNpcCard` (CP-12b) | Roster transition; NPC-Kill Penalty & Informants decides how the pawn ends |
| `BoundLive` | `NpcVacant` | Informant killed: its card is vacated like an NPC's; the informant adopts a different unobserved crowd pawn via `TakeNpcCard` | NPC-Kill Penalty & Informants |
| `BoundLive` | `NpcLive` or `NpcVacant` | Disconnect grace expired or permanent leave (CP-13b); a rejoin inside grace keeps `BoundLive` | Round / Session & Travel |
| `NpcLive` | `BoundLive` | `TakeNpcCard`: crowd adoption of an existing unobserved NPC pawn by an informant (CP-12b, CP-13c); no late join exists | Round re-admits (R18) through NPC-Kill Penalty & Informants, which picks the pawn with LSC `PickRespawnPawn` |
| any | `Discarded` | `Reset` | Round |

**Deck invariants** (hold in every state): **I-C1** `sum(c_a) = D` and each `c_a` is fixed for the round; **I-C2** the number of live pawns wearing appearance `a` is `c_a - V_a`, where `V_a` is the number of vacant cards of `a`, so exactly `c_a` when no card is `NpcVacant`; **I-C3** the transient shortfall is at most the number of in-flight vacancies (`TakeNpcCard` is an adoption: it destroys and creates no pawn and so creates no vacancy); **I-C4** the multiset of NPC cards plus bound cards is the deck, so no transition changes which appearances exist or how many of each; **I-C5** a card bound to a pawn that dies or leaves Active is never given to the same participant's next pawn by rule (a dead pawn's card is `NpcVacant`, never a candidate; a struck-out pawn is released only after the adoption), so the next look is per-pawn uniform over the unobserved crowd except the forced-NPC replacement (CP-12b).

**Pool tier states** (content, not runtime): `Tier A (mannequin variants)` -> `Tier B (Meshy models)` or `Tier C (shared body + Meshy heads)`. A tier change is a content change gated by CP-15; no runtime rule depends on the tier.

**Replication: what replicates, to whom** (all -> ADR for the exact mechanism). `AppearanceId` is **one property on the Shared Pawn class** (uint8, default `255`), replicated with an initial-only condition and a RepNotify, set through a deferred spawn so that the first replication bunch already carries it, and applied by one idempotent function called from the server's spawn, the client's initial replication and the notify (RepNotify does not fire on the listen-server authority, so the server calls the function explicitly). It is written once per pawn and the setter asserts on a second write. The value is **identical in type, path and timing for NPC and player pawns**, audience `All`, never accompanied by a participant reference, and every pawn has the same net priority, update frequency and relevancy distance. A pawn that leaves and re-enters relevancy receives the same id again.

Forbidden surfaces (Pillar 1): an appearance field on `PlayerState`; any `GameState` array keyed by participant; per-connection or per-viewer replication conditions; replicated properties, components or subobjects that exist only on player pawns; spawn, actor-name, array or relevancy ordering correlated with player-ness (spawn all `D` in one frame in a server-secret shuffled order); an RPC that carries an appearance; a `PlayerState` reference on the pawn that replicates to non-owners (CP-10); a second pawn class, archetype or subobject list for players (legacy replication does not send dynamic actor names, but it does send the archetype); net dormancy, update frequency or priority that differs by class; the first-export order of network ids correlated with player-ness (in legacy replication dynamic NetGUIDs are assigned at first export to each connection in priority order, and a connection's own pawn is boosted on that connection, -> ADR verify for legacy and Iris; AC-CP-17). The apply function must not depend on `BeginPlay`-initialised state (the initial RepNotify can precede `BeginPlay`), and a `PostNetInit`-time check asserts `AppearanceId != 255` in development. Whether the engine's `Owner` / `Controller` references reach non-owner clients for player pawns, and the Iris-specific form of all of this, are -> ADR items to verify by test (the engine reference has no Iris detail; uncertainty recorded by the replication consult). **Host trust:** the listen-server host process holds the server-only map in memory and, more simply, can enumerate every PlayerController and its pawn in-process, so every forbidden-surface rule is moot for the host; this cannot be prevented in MVP and is documented as an accepted limit (a dedicated server closes it). Pillar-1 evidence (AC-CP-15 to AC-CP-20, AC-CP-25) is gathered on **remote** clients, and claims about the host's own view are excluded. Only GameMode-side code may call the allocator; presentation code reads `Pawn->AppearanceId` only.

Bandwidth: about 1 byte of payload plus 1 to 2 bytes of handle per pawn per channel open, so roughly 60 to 180 bytes once for 60 pawns and zero steady state: this is the **marginal** cost of the appearance property only. A relevancy re-entry re-sends the pawn's whole initial bunch (transform, movement, class), not just the id. The larger cost CPA's parity rules impose is indirect: identical update frequency and priority for all `D` pawns means the 50 NPCs replicate at the player rate, roughly 60 pawns x 12 to 20 B x 30 Hz = 22 to 36 KB/s per client worst case if all are relevant, against the 50 KB/s budget (**provisional**: measure with Network Insights in a 10-client run; the owner of that budget line is Shared Character & Locomotion).

## Formulas

Notation: `P` = number of participants in the call (10 by default), `N` = `NpcCount` (50), `A` = appearances in the pool (12 MVP, at most 24), `M` = models in the pool. Counts come from the frozen TDC set and the pool table; every value is an integer. Numeric results below were checked with a pure-Python simulation (2,000-round runs, fixed seeds) in the design session; the reproduction script is a QA artefact (AC-CP-04).

### F-1 Deck size

The deck size formula is defined as:

`D = P + N`

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `P` | int | 1 to 10 (up to 2 x `TeamSize`; TDC `TeamSize` 1 to 5; 1 only in a development preset with an empty team) | Round (participant set, every rostered participant, connected or not) | Participants dealt a card |
| `N` | int | 6 to 100 (TDC hard range; safe 30 to 50) | TDC `NpcCount` | NPC pawns at round start |
| `D` | int | 7 to 110 in any build; `D_min` to 110 for a shipping-valid deal | calculated | Cards in the deck = pawns in the crowd at round start |

**Output Range:** 7 to 110 (default 60). A deal is shipping-valid only if `D >= D_min = DeckCopiesFloorCount * DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount` (24 at defaults; 15 to 72 across the hard ranges); below it the validation ladder applies (Edge Cases).
**Example:** `P = 10`, `N = 50` gives `D = 60`.

### F-2 Active appearance count

The active appearance count formula is defined as:

`K = max(1, min(A, DeckActiveAppearancesMaxCount, floor(D / DeckCopiesFloorCount)))`

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `A` | int | 12 to 24 shipping (1 to 24 dev) | pool table | Appearances available in the pool |
| `DeckActiveAppearancesMaxCount` | int | 6 to 16 (default 12) | data file (CPA key) | Upper bound on looks in play |
| `DeckCopiesFloorCount` | int | 3 to 6 (default 4) | data file (CPA key) | Minimum copies of every active appearance |
| `D` | int | 7 to 110 | F-1 | Deck size |
| `K` | int | 1 to 16 (shipping-valid: `DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount` to 16) | calculated | Looks in play this round |

**Output Range:** 6 to 12 at defaults for any shipping-valid deal. `K < DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount` fails validation in shipping-reachable presets and proceeds with `DeckBelowFloor` in development (Edge Cases); `K = 1` occurs only for `D < 2 * DeckCopiesFloorCount` in development. `K = A` holds only when `A` is also below the cap and the copies bound.
**Example:** `D = 60`, `A = 12` gives `K = min(12, 12, 15) = 12` (`D = 50` gives `min(12, 12, 12) = 12`). `D = 46` gives `K = 11`; `D = 42` gives `K = 10`; `D = 24` gives `K = 6`.

### F-3 Copies per appearance

The copies-per-appearance formula is defined as:

`q = floor(D / K)`, `r = D - q * K`, `c_a = q + 1` for the `r` appearances in the extras set `E` (a uniformly random `r`-subset of the `K` active appearances), `c_a = q` otherwise

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `q` | int | `DeckCopiesFloorCount` to `floor(D / K)` (up to 27 in a development 4-appearance pool) | calculated | Base copies |
| `r` | int | 0 to `K - 1` | calculated | Number of appearances holding one extra copy |
| `E` | set | `r` of `K` | server RNG | Which appearances hold the extra copy; **uniform**, never "the first r" (a fixed choice is a tell and is test T7) |
| `c_a` | int | `q` or `q + 1` | calculated | Copies of appearance `a`; `sum(c_a) = D`; `max - min <= 1` |

**Output Range:** `c_a >= DeckCopiesFloorCount` because `K <= floor(D / DeckCopiesFloorCount)` forces `q >= DeckCopiesFloorCount` whenever `D >= DeckCopiesFloorCount` (always true: `D >= 7 > 6`, so the `max(1, ...)` guard in F-2 and `Error_DeckTooSmall` are defensive only).
**Example:** `D = 60`, `K = 12`: `q = 5`, `r = 0`; all 12 appearances have 5 copies (`12 * 5 = 60`, the default since `NpcCount` 50, 2026-10-25). `D = 50`, `K = 12`: `q = 4`, `r = 2`; 10 appearances have 4 copies, 2 have 5 (`10 * 4 + 2 * 5 = 50`). `D = 46`, `K = 11`: `q = 4`, `r = 2` (`9 * 4 + 2 * 5`). `D = 42`, `K = 10`: `q = 4`, `r = 2`. `D = 24`, `K = 6`: `q = 4`, `r = 0` (all 4).

### F-4 Model-stratified subset draw (when `A > K`)

The subset draw is defined as the following procedure (no player input):

1. Shuffle the models uniformly; shuffle the variants inside each model.
2. Pass `j = 1, 2, ...`: for each model in shuffled order, if it has an unchosen variant and fewer than `K` appearances are chosen, choose its next variant in that model's shuffled order.
3. Stop when `K` appearances are chosen.

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `M` | int | 6 to 8 | pool table | Models |
| `v_m` | int | 1 to 3 | pool table | Variants of model `m` |
| `n_m` | int | 0 to `v_m` | calculated | Appearances chosen from model `m` |

**Output Range:** `|n_m - n_m'| <= 1` for any two models that both still have unchosen variants; every model is chosen at least once when `K >= M`. Before the swatch filter each appearance of an 8 x 3 pool has inclusion probability `K / A = 1/2`; after it, the exact value comes from the enumeration (it stays equal across appearances of the same `VariantKind` by symmetry of the swatch rule, and is team- and player-independent in every case).
**Example:** `M = 8`, `v_m = 3`, `K = 12`: all 8 models appear, 4 uniformly random models contribute a second variant. MVP pool (`M = 6`, `A = 12`, `K = 12`): the subset is the whole pool, nothing is drawn.

**Swatch floor (revised in design review 2026-10-24).** The subset must contain at least `SwatchFloor = min(5, K, M)` distinct garment swatches. Re-drawing is **not** used: at the MVP pool with `K = 6` only 32 of the 64 stratified subsets reach 5 swatches, so a capped re-draw made about 0.39 percent of small rounds invalid, and `K <= 4` could never reach 5. Instead the subset is drawn **uniformly from the valid stratified subsets**: the allocator enumerates the stratified patterns (which variant of each model, and which models take an extra variant; at most a few thousand for an 8 x 3 pool, offline-checkable), filters those meeting `SwatchFloor`, and picks one uniformly with the `Deal` stream. A pool with no valid subset for the needed `K` fails validation (`DeckInvalid`), which the pool validator detects at editor time for every reachable `K` (5 to 16). Inclusion probabilities are then those of the uniform valid-subset law, computed exactly by the same enumeration (AC-CP-02), not assumed to be `K / A`.

### F-5 Players per appearance, twin anonymity and team blindness

The players-per-appearance distribution is defined as:

`X_a ~ Hypergeometric(D, c_a, P)`: `Pr[X_a = k] = C(c_a, k) * C(D - c_a, P - k) / C(D, P)`; mean `P * c_a / D`; and for any pawn wearing `a`, `Pr[pawn is a participant's | a, all c] = P / D` for every `a`.

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `X_a` | int | 0 to `min(c_a, P)` | calculated | Participants holding appearance `a` |
| `P / D` | float | 0.167 at default | calculated | Probability that any one card is a participant's |
| `T_a = c_a - 1` | int | 4 at default | calculated | Twins of a pawn wearing `a` |

**Output Range / Example** (`P = 10`, `N = 50`, `D = 60`, every `c = 5`): `Pr[X = 0..5] = .388, .422, .161, .027, .0019, .00005`. A participant has 4 twins; the chance that all 4 are also participants is `C(9,4) / C(59,4) = 0.00028`. The expected number of NPC twins of a participant is 3.39. Two specific participants share a look with probability `sum_a c_a (c_a - 1) / (D (D - 1)) = 240 / 3540 = 0.0678` (identical for teammates and rivals; expected shared pairs among 10 participants: `45 * 0.0678 = 3.05`, of which `20 * 0.0678 = 1.36` are teammate pairs). At F-8's reference configuration (`N = 40`, `D = 50`): `c = 4`: `Pr[X = 0..4] = .397, .429, .152, .021, .0009`; `c = 5`: `Pr[X = 0..5] = .311, .431, .210, .044, .004, .0001`; 3 or 4 twins; `160 / 2450 = 0.0653`.

**Team-blindness proof.** (1) The counts `c_a` are a function of `(D, A, pool, server RNG)` only. (2) The allocator input is the participant id set; its type has no team field, so the output distribution is the same for every team labelling. (3) Fisher-Yates with rejection sampling makes the assignment of the `D` cards to the `D` pawn slots a uniform permutation, so the deck is exchangeable: for any fixed rule mapping participants to deck positions, the joint law of `(a_1..a_P)` is the law of `P` draws without replacement, hence invariant under any relabelling of participants, including by team. (4) Spawn order, ids and placement are independent of the card (CP-10), so the set of participant pawns is uniform over the pawns an observer can see. (5) Therefore the likelihood ratio for "this pawn is a participant" given its appearance, every `c_a` and any other pawn's known status is exactly 1: given that one pawn is known to be a participant, every other pawn (twin or not) is a participant with probability `(P - 1) / (D - 1) = 9/59`, the same for every appearance. **The proof fails** if the output depends on the participant set, if the participant slots are not label-uniform (spawn order, NetGUID, array index, "players spawn first"), if the random stream is reconstructible, or if any later step re-sorts or conditions on appearance; each of these has a test (F-8).

### F-6 Replacement draw and conservation

The replacement draw is defined as:

`Pr[draw card v] = 1 / |V|` for `v` in the vacant NPC set `V`; ledger `L_a + V_a = c_a` at all times

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `V` | set | 0 to `N` | server ledger | NPC cards in state `NpcVacant` |
| `L_a` | int | 0 to `c_a` | server ledger | Live pawns wearing `a` (bound and NPC); "live" = strike role not `Dead`: a killed body still in the world holds no card, because `VacateCard` runs in the kill slot (ADR-0018 §6.4) |
| `V_a` | int | 0 to `c_a` | server ledger | Vacant NPC cards of appearance `a` |

**Output Range:** with no vacancy, `L_a = c_a` for every `a`, so the original counts and the spread `<= 1` are restored exactly; with `n` outstanding vacancies `L_a >= c_a - n`.
**Example:** appearance `a` has `c_a = 4`. Its NPC copy is assassinated: `V = {a}`, `Pr[a] = 1`, so the replacement wears `a` and counts return to 4. A participant's pawn of look `a` dies: `V = {a}` exactly as for an NPC, and the participant adopts a uniformly chosen unobserved NPC pawn (say of look `m`), whose card moves `NpcLive -> BoundLive`, so `a` is refilled and `m` is worn by the informant: counts unchanged. If two NPCs of `a` and `b` die before either replacement arrives: `V = {a, b}`, each draw is `a` or `b` with probability 1/2 and after both draws the counts are restored. A **fresh** uniform draw from the active set is rejected: counts would random-walk and break `DeckCopiesFloorCount` within a few dozen kills.

### F-7 Swatch assignment

The swatch assignment formula is defined as:

`swatch(k, o) = ((k - 1 + o) mod N_models) + 1`; material index `= swatch - 1`. Garment: `o = 0` (Default, Accessory) or `o = 1` (GarmentSwap); trim: `o = 3` in every variant

| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `k` | int | 1 to `N_models` | pool table order | Model number |
| `N_models` | int | 6 to 8 | pool table | Number of models = number of active swatches |
| `o` | int | 0, 1, 3 | art bible 4.3 | Offset for default garment, swap garment, trim |

**Output Range:** swatch 1 to `N_models`, material index 0 to 7. Each swatch appears on exactly 3 distinct models (as default, swap, trim), for every `N_models` from 6 to 8.
**Example:** `N_models = 6`, model `k = 5`: default garment `S5`, swap garment `S6`, trim `S((5 - 1 + 3) mod 6 + 1) = S2`. Model `k = 6`: swap garment `S1` (wraps), trim `S3`.

### F-8 Statistical indistinguishability test (gate G-DECK)

The test is defined for the reference configuration `P = 10`, `N = 40`, MVP pool (`A = 12`, `K = 12`; `D = 50`, `r = 2`, kept off the shipping default on purpose: at `NpcCount` 50 the default deck `D = 60` has `r = 0`, so T1, T7, B1 and B4 would be vacuous; T2-T6 may also be run at `D = 60`), `R = 2000` simulated allocator calls with **fixed, pre-registered seeds** (a failing seed is a defect, never reseeded). The suite evaluates 8 statistics (T1, T2 twice, T3b, T4 to T7; T3a is deterministic), each at `alpha = 0.001` (`abs(z) > 3.291`, `chi2(11) > 31.264`), so a correct allocator fails the suite with probability about 0.8 percent on a fixed seed set; the seed set is a checked-in file and is chosen once. Every allocator draw uses the server-secret stream `FSecretStream` (SipHash-2-4 in counter mode, ADR-0006 §5; tags `CPA_DEAL`/`CPA_REPL`/`CPA_TAKE`), never an engine random stream whose algorithm may change between engine versions; golden vectors pin it. Offline validation of the harness (a separate nightly test, `Deck.HarnessCalibration`, at the suite's `alpha = 0.001`) over 100 other seed sets must show **no more than 4** suite failures (expected 0.8; "at most 2" would fail a correct harness about 4.7 percent of the time, "at most 4" about 0.15 percent). Design-time check at `alpha = 0.01`: T1 0/100, T2 0/100, T3b 1/100, about the nominal 1 percent. **Null corrections** (systems-designer review): T7 draws `r` distinct appearances per round without replacement, so its raw statistic is distributed as `((K - r) / (K - 1)) * chi2(K - 1)`; the harness multiplies it by `(K - 1) / (K - r)` (11/10 at defaults) before comparing with 31.264. T3b's within-round sampling without replacement inflates the statistic by about 2 percent (true alpha about 0.0012); it is run as a per-round permutation test (team labels permuted within each round, 10,000 permutations, fixed seed).

| Test | Statistic | Null expectation | Reject if | Catches |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| **T1 copy-count leak** | `Y` = participants holding a larger-count (`q + 1`) card per round, summed; `z = (sum Y - 2R) / sqrt(1.306 R)` (`Y ~ Hyper(50, 10, 10)`, mean 2, variance 1.306) | `z ~ N(0,1)` | `abs(z) > 3.291` | Frequency of an appearance carries information about being a participant (the retired rule; weighted dealing) |
| **T2 uniformity** | chi-square of the appearance dealt to participant slot 0, and again slot 9, over `R` rounds | expected `R / 12 = 167` per cell, 11 degrees of freedom | `chi2 > 31.264` | Biased dealing by position or appearance |
| **T3 team-blind** | (a) metamorphic: same seed with team labels flipped or permuted gives a **byte-identical** deal; (b) chi-square of the 2 x 12 table (team A slots 0 to 4, team B slots 5 to 9) by appearance | 11 degrees of freedom | any (a) difference, or (b) `chi2 > 31.264` | Any team dependence |
| **T4 order** | adjacent-equal pairs in the `NpcAppearances` list: mean per round | `39 * 160 / 2450 = 2.547` | z-test with the sample SD, `abs(z) > 3.291` | Sorted or grouped NPC output |
| **T5 distinct looks among participants** | distinct appearances among the 10 participants | mean 7.41 (`2 * .689 + 10 * .603`); z-test with the sample SD (about 1.0) | `abs(z) > 3.291` | A "participants wear distinct looks" rule (the retired rule gives 10.0) |
| **T6 teammate sharing** | same-team pairs sharing a look per round | `20 * 0.0653 = 1.31`; z-test with the sample SD | `abs(z) > 3.291` | A rule that forbids teammates from sharing (that rule would itself leak) |
| **T7 extras uniformity** | chi-square of which appearances received the extra copy over `R * r` draws, scaled by `(K - 1) / (K - r)` | expected `R * r / 12 = 333` per cell, 11 degrees of freedom after scaling | scaled `chi2 > 31.264` | "First r" extras |

**Negative controls (must be rejected by the harness, run in CI as tests of the test).** B1: participants weighted x2 toward 5-copy cards (measured `z = 46`, T1). B2: the retired rule, distinct participant looks plus at least 3 NPC duplicates (T1 `z = -9.3`, T5 mean 10.0). B3: teammates forbidden from sharing (T1 `z = -5.7`, T5 mean 8.08, T6 mean 0.0). B4: extras always on appearances 0 and 1 (T7 is the deterministic detector; T2 reached `chi2 = 27.5` on one seed and is not relied on). **Power:** against a milder bias (participants 1.1x as likely to hold 5-copy cards) a two-by-two chi-square at `alpha = 0.01` rejected 99 percent of 2,000-round runs and 57 percent of 500-round runs; at `alpha = 0.001` T1 detects a shift of 0.117 in mean `Y` per round (0.200 to 0.212 per participant, about 6 percent relative) with power 0.9 at `R = 2000`. **Sample size:** `R = 2000` calls of a 50-card deal are milliseconds in an automation test (no engine needed: the allocator is a pure function).

### F-9 Residency and bandwidth (constraint, not a tuning formula)

`bytes_once ~ D * (1 + h)` with `h` = 1 to 2 bytes of handle overhead; `D = 60` gives 120 to 180 bytes per client once; steady state 0. The 50 KB/s budget is untouched (**provisional** until profiled).

## Edge Cases

**Deal and validation**

- **If `AllocateAppearances` is called a second time in one round**: the second call returns `Error_AlreadyDealt` and changes nothing (one-shot). The next call is legal only after `Reset`.
- **If `D < DeckCopiesFloorCount`**: unreachable under TDC's hard ranges (`D >= 7 > 6`); kept as a defensive refusal (`Error_DeckTooSmall`).
- **If `K < DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount` (6), including `D < D_min` (24) or a pool with too few usable appearances**: the allocator's check on the real `D` and pool is authoritative (TDC's I-3 cannot see a participant set smaller than `2 * TeamSize`, the pool size or the subset law). The outcome is decided by an injected **floor policy**: `Enforce` (the only policy compiled into Shipping, and used for `Default` and `HostSelectable` presets in every build) fails the deal, Round returns to `Lobby` and logs `DeckInvalid` (same ladder as TDC's `TuningLoadFailed`; the deal is never patched). `Warn` (development and test builds with a non-selectable test preset, for example `Solo`) proceeds with the largest feasible `K`, logs `DeckBelowFloor` in the Round Event Log header, and the round is excluded from Pillar-1 evidence. (Twin anonymity falls below the target: at `D = 24`, `P = 10` the chance that all 3 twins of a participant are also participants is 4.7 percent.)
- **If `A` is below both the cap and the copies bound** (`A < min(DeckActiveAppearancesMaxCount, floor(D / DeckCopiesFloorCount))`): `K = A` and copies per appearance exceed the minimum (e.g. `A = 8`, `D = 50` gives `K = 8`, `q = 6`, `r = 2`, development only since 8 is below the MVP floor). If `A` exceeds the cap, the cap binds (e.g. `A = 14`, `D = 110` gives `K = 12`).
- **If `r = 0`**: every active appearance has exactly `q` copies; the extras set is empty.
- **If a model has fewer variants than the draw needs**: the round-robin skips that model (F-4); counts differ by more than 1 only when a model has no unchosen variant left.
- **If a Tier C pool (shared body, Meshy heads) or Tier B pool replaces Tier A mid-development**: no rule changes; `AppearanceId` values of existing rows are stable (CP-1), new rows take unused ids, and the pool gate re-runs (CP-15).
- **If no stratified subset meets `SwatchFloor = min(5, K, M)`**: the deal fails validation as above (`DeckInvalid`); there is no re-draw loop (F-4 draws uniformly from valid subsets). The pool validator reports this at editor time for every reachable `K`.
- **If a client's `PoolHash` differs from the server's, or its single round acknowledgement does not arrive within Session's `TravelLoadTimeoutSec`**: Session's rules apply (resend once, then `ParamsMismatch`; or `Disconnected(TravelTimeout)`); CPA has no timer or Reason of its own. The participant still gets its bound, AI-driven pawn at P6 (Round), so nothing is missing from the crowd.

**Replacement, kills and respawn**

- **If an NPC is killed**: its card becomes `NpcVacant`; Crowd draws a replacement from the vacant set (F-6); the replacement wears a vacated appearance, so counts return to `c_a` once all vacancies are filled.
- **If two NPCs are killed before either replacement spawns (simultaneous or overlapping)**: both cards are vacant; each draw is uniform over the remaining vacant cards; order of kills (Round's total order) is irrelevant to the final counts.
- **If the vacancy backlog exceeds 3, or a vacancy is older than Crowd's `CrowdReplacementMaxDelaySec` (Crowd F3)**: Crowd spawns the next replacement at once (the oldest vacancy's timer governs **when**, never **which look**) and logs `VacancyBacklog`; the look is still the uniform `DrawReplacement` over all vacant cards; the transient shortfall is at most the backlog (I-C3). Replacement delay and socket pool must not depend on whether the victim was an NPC, a player or an informant (CP-12 interaction row).
- **If an active player is assassinated (not reversed)**: the victim pawn dies and its card becomes `NpcVacant` (a replacement NPC later emerges with a look drawn by `DrawReplacement()`, uniform over all vacant NPC cards (CP-12a, Crowd CNB-R9.2), exactly as for an NPC victim; not necessarily the victim's look); the participant then adopts an existing unobserved crowd pawn via `TakeNpcCard` (no spawn, no emergence), whose look is per-pawn uniform over the unobserved crowd except the forced-NPC replacement (CP-12b). The killer therefore cannot carry a look forward, and the only emergence an observer could ever notice is the one replacement that also follows an NPC kill.
- **If an informant is killed**: its card becomes `NpcVacant` (the replacement NPC's look is drawn by `DrawReplacement()`, uniform over all vacant NPC cards, CP-12a, Crowd CNB-R9.2; cross-review S-I3 2026-10-24) and the participant adopts a different unobserved crowd pawn (CP-12b). If the participant has disconnected meanwhile, no pawn is adopted and the participant waits as "awaiting pawn" (Roster R11) until Round re-admits it (R18).
- **If a defender's reversal eliminates the attacker**: the attacker's pawn dies or is released to the crowd (NPC-Kill Penalty & Informants decides which; either way its card goes to the NPC side; a release happens at the elimination and the released body is excluded from the adoption pick) and the attacker adopts a different crowd pawn; the defender learns nothing about the attacker's future look.
- **If a participant is struck out (third NPC kill) and its pawn survives**: `ReleaseToCrowd` turns the old pawn into an NPC with its card at the exile; at `T_due` the participant adopts a different pawn picked by `PickRespawnPawn` with the released body as `ExcludePawn`. The participant can never re-adopt its own released pawn (reconciliation 2026-10-24, Penalty CR-6).
- **If two informants need a pawn in the same tick**: Round's total order fixes the sequence; each `TakeNpcCard` draws from the candidates not yet taken (without replacement); a request left without a candidate waits for the CP-13c retry.
- **If an NPC kill and an informant adoption resolve in the same Round tick**: Round's total order fixes the sequence; `TakeNpcCard` considers only `NpcLive` cards held by existing pawns (never `NpcVacant`), so the two cannot exchange cards, and a card taken by the informant is never also returned by `DrawReplacement`.
- **If a replacement is requested while `V` is empty (a Crowd bug)**: `DrawReplacement` returns `Error_NoVacancy`, spawns nothing and logs `LedgerMismatch`; the ledger invariant I-C1 is asserted in development builds.
- **If a pawn is destroyed by something that is not a kill (kill-plane, a bug)**: an NPC pawn's card becomes `NpcVacant` and is refilled by `DrawReplacement`. An `Active` participant's pawn gets Round's own-card respawn (R17.9): the card stays `BoundLive` and one pawn of the same `AppearanceId` emerges out of non-team sight (CP-11). An informant's pawn vacates its card like an NPC's, and Penalty re-pawns the informant at once by adoption (Penalty CR-8). (Reconciliation 2026-10-24, Round CR 15.)

**Disconnect, join and rejoin**

- **If a player disconnects during `Preparing`, before pawns spawn**: Round's rule decides whether the round starts; CPA's deal is not recomputed (`Players` already contains every rostered participant, connected or not); the participant still gets its bound pawn at P6, AI-driven by the Crowd controller, the card stays `BoundLive`, and the grace clock starts at `ActivePhaseStart` (Round P6 and R17; CP-13a).
- **If a player disconnects during `Active` and rejoins within `DisconnectGraceSec`**: the same pawn and card are restored (CP-13a).
- **If the grace expires or a player leaves permanently**: informant transition then `ReleaseToCrowd`; the pawn stays as an NPC with the same look; `D`, `c_a` unchanged. An observer sees the same pawn keep walking (no vanishing).
- **If a new person tries to join mid-round**: Session rejects it (`RejoinOnly`, `InProgress`); CPA is never called and no card moves.
- **If a returning informant (re-admitted after grace expiry, Round R18) needs a pawn and no candidate is unobserved**: NPC-Kill Penalty & Informants waits and re-picks per LSC R5b.5 (`LateJoinTakeoverTimeoutSec` does not apply to informants, Penalty CR-7); the card ledger is untouched until a pawn is picked.
- **If an adoption would reduce the effective NPC count below `2 * TeamSize`**: Round refuses the re-admission (TDC I-2); the card ledger is untouched.
- **If the listen-server host leaves**: the session ends for everyone (no migration in MVP); the next session deals afresh.
- **If a team is smaller than 5 (test presets, `TeamSize` below 5)**: nothing changes except `P`; `K` follows F-2, and if `D < 24` the validation rules above apply.

**Information leaks (Pillar 1)**

- **If anything would let a client derive the deal (the seed from the layout seed, the round id or the tick count; a logged seed; a replicated deck composition)**: forbidden by CP-9; the deal digest is the only logged value, and the deck is not in client-visible replays until `Ended`.
- **If a pawn exists on a client before `AppearanceId` resolves**: the pawn draws nothing (CP-18); a default look, the sentinel `255` or a placeholder mesh is never visible for any frame, for any pawn class.
- **If an appearance's assets are not yet resident when its first pawn spawns**: unreachable by construction (all pool entries are resident before the round leaves `Preparing`, CP-18); if it happens in development, the round logs `PoolNotResident` and is excluded from evidence.
- **If a rule would make teammates look different (or alike) on purpose, or forbid a repeat of last round's look**: forbidden (leak, B3 and the Roster history request).
- **If a replacement emergence would be within line of sight of any opposing viewer**: it is deferred until an out-of-sight shared emergence socket is free (Crowd), never performed in view and never moved to a team-specific socket; informants never emerge at all (they adopt).
- **If a spawn-spacing rule keeps twins apart for NPCs only**: forbidden; a spacing rule, if any, applies to all `D` pawns identically (players are never exempt), and is a Crowd decision.
- **If the owner's first-person body is hidden or an own-pawn aid is requested**: allowed only as a local render flag or camera framing (owner-no-see); no outline, highlight, tint or marker on the owner's pawn visible to anyone (Pillar 1); a new player's difficulty finding their own pawn is a playtest question solved by camera framing, not by an appearance difference.
- **If the Visibility Policy is asked `CanViewerKnow(Viewer, Subject, AppearanceId)`**: always `true`. If asked "which participant owns this pawn's look": `false` for every viewer except `PostRound` where Round chooses to release it.
- **If a future cosmetic, accessibility option or debug tool changes one pawn's look for one viewer**: rejected by CP-16 and CP-17; development-only debug views that do so must be compiled out of shipping.
- **If the host-side process inspects the ledger**: accepted limit (Interactions); a dedicated server closes it.
- **If the round ends**: cards are `Discarded`; `DealDigest` and the active set stay in the Round Event Log, which Round may release at `Ended` under the Visibility Policy.

## Tuning Knobs

Keys follow the TDC Knob Registry Convention (CR-15 of `tuning-data-config.md`; revised in design review 2026-10-24: `Min`/`Max` appear only in the range pair, so the copies key is a `Floor`). They are the keys TDC's provisional I-3 names by role: `MinCopies` = `DeckCopiesFloorCount`, `MinAppearances` = `DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount`. The pool table itself is **content**, not tuning (art-controlled, versioned by `PoolHash`); the keys below govern only how the deck is built. **`AppearancePalette`** (the prototype property in TDC's migration table) is owned by this system as **pool content**: its colours become the art bible 4.3 swatch values inside the static material instances of the pool table, versioned by `PoolHash`; it is not a tuning key and never a per-pawn or runtime parameter (CP-1, CP-16). All deck keys are `ServerOnly` (clients need none: the deal reaches them only as per-pawn `AppearanceId`).

| Key | Type | Owner | Default | Hard range | Safe range | Audience | Latch | Unit | Effect / what breaks |
| ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- |
| `DeckCopiesFloorCount` | int | Character Pool & Appearance | 4 | 3 to 6 | 4 to 5 | ServerOnly | Round | count | Minimum copies of every active appearance, hence the twins a participant has (`copies - 1`). **Too low:** a participant is trackable by look ("the one in the red coat"), anonymity set shrinks (at 3 copies, `Pr[both twins are participants]` rises). **Too high:** `K` falls (`floor(D / copies)`), the crowd reads as clones (art bible 1.3.1 immersion failure). Interacts with `DeckActiveAppearancesMaxCount` and `NpcCount` through F-2. **Tell review (CR-15.8):** sets crowd composition at the deal only, identical for every pawn; no timing or player-specific effect; not a tell. |
| `DeckActiveAppearancesMaxCount` | int | Character Pool & Appearance | 12 | 6 to 16 | 8 to 12 | ServerOnly | Round | count | Upper bound on distinct looks in play. **Too low:** fewer looks, more clones. **Too high:** with `D = 60` a cap of 16 would leave 3 copies of some looks, so the `DeckCopiesFloorCount` bound (not this key) takes over and values above `floor(D / DeckCopiesFloorCount)` = 15 are inert at default `NpcCount`. Cannot usefully exceed the pool's appearance count. Range pair with `DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount` (TDC I-5 enforces Min <= Max). **Tell review (CR-15.8):** composition only; not a tell. |
| `DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount` | int | Character Pool & Appearance | 6 | 5 to 12 | 6 to 8 | ServerOnly | Round | count | Validation floor on `K`: below it the deal fails (`Enforce` policy) or warns (`Warn` policy, development test presets). Sets the smallest legal deck `D_min = DeckCopiesFloorCount * DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount` (24 at defaults). Hard minimum 5 (raised in design review) so the swatch floor `min(5, K, M)` is always 5 for a valid deal. **Too low:** tiny crowds pass validation and Pillar 1 is formal only. **Too high:** presets with fewer NPCs are rejected. **Tell review (CR-15.8):** validation only; not a tell. |

`PoolResidentTimeoutSec` (previously listed here) is **retired** (design review 2026-10-24): pool residency rides Session & Travel's single round acknowledgement, bounded by Session's `TravelLoadTimeoutSec` (CP-18).

**Contract constants (not runtime keys; changing them is a design and art-bible change):** pool size 6 to 8 models, at most 24 appearances, MVP floor 12 appearances (6 models x 2 variants), swatch offsets `0 / 1 / 3` (art bible 4.3), sentinel `255`, hypergeometric test thresholds in F-8.

**Invariants for TDC's list** (change request; revised in design review 2026-10-24): **I-3 (replaces the provisional I-3)** `2 * TeamSize + NpcCount >= DeckCopiesFloorCount * DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount` (24 at defaults), **scoped like I-4**: a CI failure and a runtime rejection for `Default` and `HostSelectable` presets (the only presets Shipping can reach), a **warning** in non-selectable test presets (for example `Solo`, `TeamSize` 1), where CPA's `Warn` floor policy applies. TDC's `NpcCount` hard minimum of 6 then rests on I-2 alone, and TDC AC-17's "`TeamSize` 1, `NpcCount` 10 passes" becomes "passes with a warning". `DeckActiveAppearancesMinCount <= DeckActiveAppearancesMaxCount` is covered by TDC's existing range-pair invariant I-5 (no new invariant; the earlier CPA "I-5" is withdrawn because TDC already uses that number). TDC's invariant is necessary but not sufficient: the allocator's own check on the real `D`, pool and subset law stays authoritative (Edge Cases).

**Keys owned elsewhere that this system relies on** (never restated): `NpcCount`, `TeamSize` (TDC); `DisconnectGraceSec` (Round, default 30), `LateJoinTakeoverTimeoutSec` (Round, default 30; used only for informant re-pawn retries); `TravelLoadTimeoutSec` (Session & Travel); `CrowdReplacementMinDelaySec`/`CrowdReplacementMaxDelaySec` and the replacement delay distribution (Crowd F3; cross-review C-W15 2026-10-24).

**Interactions.** Raising `DeckCopiesFloorCount` lowers `K` and raises the minimum `NpcCount` that the pool can honour; raising `NpcCount` raises `K` until the `DeckActiveAppearancesMaxCount` cap, then raises copies. The **playtest grid** (OQ-4) is `(copies, max looks)` = `(3, 16)`, `(4, 12)` (default), `(6, 8)`; observe track-loss rate after a 5 s occlusion, how often three or more same-look characters are in one frame, unprompted "clone" comments, whether players chose a target because of its look, and the AC-CP-25 participant-share metric.

## Visual/Audio Requirements

*Shaped by an art-director consult. The art bible (`design/art/art-bible.md`) remains the authority for every look; this section states what CPA requires of it and where the two documents disagree.*

**Appearance to material mapping (art bible 4.3, 8.x.6).** An `AppearanceId` resolves to one pool row: a skeletal mesh and a **static** `MI_Char_<Model>_<Variant>` instance of `M_Character_Master` with constant `GarmentSwatchIndex` and `TrimSwatchIndex` (rule F-7; material index = swatch minus 1). No runtime dynamic material instance exists for pool characters, so nothing can be set per pawn, per team or per viewer (8.x.6 "no keyed branches": no parameter, switch or function named `Team`, `Role`, `IsPlayer`, `IsNPC`, `Ally`). Trim is mandatory in every variant at no more than 25 percent of garment area (4.3); `SwatchMaskTex` therefore needs separate garment and trim regions, which puts the master at its cap of 4 texture samples with no headroom (technical-artist to confirm).

**Identical for every pawn class and every viewer** (1.3.5, 5.2): the same skeletal mesh and material instance per `AppearanceId`; LOD by screen size only; the same skin-cache opt-in, Nanite setting, physics asset, animation tick rate and fidelity, shadow, reflection and Lumen response; no custom depth or stencil, outline, tint, per-pawn VFX, footstep or dust difference. No placeholder or default look is drawn for even one frame before `AppearanceId` resolves. A look is re-created (NPC replacement) only out of sight of every viewer (5.2.1 rule 1); an informant never re-appears as a new figure, it adopts an existing crowd pawn (CP-12b).

**Twins.** Twins are guaranteed (every active appearance has at least 4 copies), so copies must be perfectly interchangeable: no per-instance random tint, scale, dirt or wetness; variation comes from behaviour, animation phase and gaze only (Crowd). The allocator never clusters or separates twins by team or participant (that is a signal). Gate **G-TWIN**: 4 copies of one appearance within 6 m, captured at 15 m and at close range, read as an ordinary crowd.

**Per-match subset (F-4, CP-19).** Every base model appears at least once when `K >= M`; the remaining picks spread across distinct models before any model doubles; at least `min(5, K, M)` distinct garment swatches are active (uniform over valid subsets, no re-draw); one subset per round, identical for both teams. With the MVP pool (12 of 12) the subset is the whole pool; with 8 x 3 it is 8 plus 4. Any subset inherits validity from the pool tests because 1.3.1 requires every model x variant combination to be individually valid.

**Pool gates (CP-15).** T1 per model: items 1 to 10 and 13 to 18 are per-model; items 11 (swatch on at least 3 models) and 19 (accessory on at least 2 other models) are pool-relative and re-run on any membership change. Adding or cutting a model changes `N_models` and so every model's swatch indices (4.3 "re-balances by changing N"): T1 items 10 to 12 and T2 re-run for **all** models, which is why `AppearanceId` is a stable key (CP-1). A material-instance-only change (the garment swap) re-runs T1 items 10 to 13 and T2; a geometry change (an accessory variant, a new head) runs the full T1 to T4 for that mesh, and T3 is per appearance, not per model. T5 re-runs on any pool, LOD or material change, plus one run with real allocator output. G-VAR (minimum variety) and G-DECK (the F-8 suite) are CPA's additions.

**MVP tier honesty (CP-5).** A pool of six mannequin variants is the 1.3.1 fallback kit invoked early: Manny and Quinn differ in build, so Quinn is not a differentiator (it would fail checklist items 1 to 5), and the stock mannequin probably fails checklist items 15 and 16 (material slots, UV density, LOD0 triangle count) until measured. Mannequins have no faces; "models" differ in hair, headwear and garment-cut geometry and each is baked as one merged single-slot mesh; mannequin grey is outside the skin band so skin and hair are authored through the master. **Risk (art-director):** the variety reads at 20 to 30 m but not at assassination range, where faceless twins look like dolls. G-VAR exists so that a pool which passes T1 to T5 only by being identical cannot ship (T1 to T5 test that no model stands out, not that models differ). **Provisional pending the Meshy retarget spike.**

**Audio.** None. The pool defines no per-appearance audio and art bible 5.2 forbids per-pawn audio differences; `AppearanceId` must not be an input to any audio selection (footsteps, cloth, voice). Diegetic Audio (22) and the audio-director own the rest.

**Asset Spec flag.** Visual requirements are defined; after the art bible is approved, run `/asset-spec system:character-pool-appearance` to produce per-asset specs (pool models, variants, accessory meshes) and generation prompts.

## Game Feel

**Not applicable as a player-operated feel target.** CPA has no input, no response time, no animation and no hit timing; the player never operates it, so there is no responsiveness, weight or snap to tune. (The category is `Core`, not `Gameplay` or `UI`, where Game Feel is mandatory.) Two feel-adjacent targets are stated so they are not lost:

- **No pop, no hitch (Pillar 1):** no character appears with a default or wrong look, and no look is slower to appear than another (CP-18; AC-CP-15). The felt result is a crowd that is simply there.
- **Finding yourself in a crowd of twins:** the player must locate their own pawn quickly after spawn or occlusion without any appearance difference. Rule: **the camera is the identifier** (the third-person camera pivots on the pawn at a fixed offset, first-person has no body to find, and movement response to input confirms "that one is me"); an own-pawn highlight, outline or tint is forbidden because it is a rendering difference the player did not choose and normalises outlines. Playtest target: a new player locates their pawn within 2 s of spawn or of leaving occlusion; if longer, fix camera framing, never the appearance (owner: Camera System, OQ-6).

## UI Requirements

**None in MVP (not applicable).** There is no appearance picker, no preview, no look name, no per-look HUD element, no outline or marker keyed to a look, and no debug overlay in shipping builds. Reasons: any UI that names or marks a look is an identification aid (anti-pillar "not an identification-tools game") and any chosen look is a Pillar-1 leak (CP-17). Boundaries with other UI systems:

- The **teammate marker** is a separate UI element (Teammate Markers, #17) and never changes a pawn's look.
- The **assassination prompt** looks and appears identically for any in-range pawn whatever its appearance (art bible 5.2; HUD / Assassination own it).
- The **post-round reveal** (Vertical Slice, #21) may show each participant's look after the round under the Visibility Policy audience `PostRound`; it must not reveal anything during `Active`.
- A **development-only** overlay that prints `AppearanceId` and card state may exist, compiled out of shipping.

## Acceptance Criteria

*Revised in design review 2026-10-24 (qa-lead, systems-designer, ue-replication-specialist findings).* Test names use the form `StealthGame.CharacterPool.<Scenario>`; automation tests live in `Source/StealthGameplay/Private/Tests/CharacterPool/`, and the editor-only pool validator tests (AC-CP-21, AC-CP-22) in `Source/StealthGameEditor/Private/Tests/CharacterPool/` (module layout of ADR-0007; multi-client tests in `Source/StealthTestHarness/Private/`, ADR-0008). "Allocator test" = a pure automation test with no world: the allocator is a pure function with an injected `IEntropySource` and the keyed stream generator of CP-9. "2-client PIE" = a listen server plus one remote client in PIE, network emulation off unless stated; every Pillar-1 assertion is made on the **remote** client (host trust limit, Replication). Story types per `.claude/docs/coding-standards.md`: Logic = automated test (BLOCKING), Integration = automated test or documented playtest (BLOCKING), Visual = retained screenshots in `production/qa/evidence/` plus lead sign-off (BLOCKING). **Seeds** for statistical tests are a `constexpr` array in `CharacterPoolTestSeeds.h` beside the tests (chosen once, never reseeded; no file I/O); each suite seed starts one stream that produces all `R` deals in sequence. **Shipping-only properties** (no seed-injection hook, no debug overlay, `Enforce` floor policy) cannot be checked by an automation test inside Shipping; they are checked by the CI step `CI.ShippingSymbolAudit`, which builds Shipping and fails if the symbols `CharacterPool_InjectEntropy` or `CharacterPool_DebugOverlay` are present or the `Warn` policy is linked. **Blocked by** marks a criterion that cannot run until the named GDD exists; it stays BLOCKING for the story that implements it. **Smoke set** (fast, deterministic, no world, no humans): AC-CP-01, 03, 08a, 09, 10, 21 and `Net.LifetimePropsAllowlist` (class default object only); the first three to run are **AC-CP-01, AC-CP-10, `Net.LifetimePropsAllowlist`**.

**Deck and allocator (Logic)**

- **AC-CP-01 (CP-6, CP-7, F-1 to F-3)** GIVEN `(P, N)` = `(10,50)` and an MVP pool of 12 appearances, WHEN `AllocateAppearances` runs for each of 1,000 seeds, THEN `D = 60`, `K = 12`, every appearance has exactly 5 copies (`r = 0`, empty extras set), `sum = 60`. Also `(10,40)` -> `K, q, r = 12, 4, 2` (2 appearances with 5 copies, 10 with 4); `(10,36)` -> `K, q, r = 11, 4, 2`; `(10,32)` -> `10, 4, 2`; `(10,14)` -> `6, 4, 0` (no `DeckInvalid` on any seed: the swatch floor is met by construction, F-4). Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.CountsBalanced`.
- **AC-CP-01b (Edge Cases, `Warn` policy)** GIVEN the `Warn` floor policy (development, non-selectable preset) and `(2,10)` or a development pool of 8 appearances with `D = 50`, THEN `(2,10)` -> `K, q, r = 3, 4, 0` with `DeckBelowFloor` in the log header and `SwatchFloor = 3`, and the 8-appearance pool -> `K = 8`, `q = 6`, `r = 2` with `PoolBelowMvpFloor`. GIVEN the `Enforce` policy and the same inputs, THEN `DeckInvalid`. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.FloorPolicyBranches`.
- **AC-CP-02 (CP-19, F-4)** GIVEN a pool of 8 models x 3 variants, WHEN the deck is built over 1,000 seeds with `K = 12`, THEN every model appears at least once, 4 models contribute 2 appearances and 4 contribute 1, at least 5 distinct garment swatches are active, and each appearance's inclusion frequency matches the exact probability computed by enumerating the valid-subset law (binomial interval at 99.9 percent, Bonferroni-corrected over 24 appearances). GIVEN the MVP 6 x 2 pool and `K = 6`, THEN over 1,000 seeds no deal fails and each of the 32 valid subsets occurs with frequency 1/32 (chi-square, df 31, `alpha = 0.001`). GIVEN a stub pool with no valid subset for the needed `K`, THEN `DeckInvalid` and the validator reports it. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.StratifiedSubset`.
- **AC-CP-02b (F-4)** GIVEN a pool whose models have unequal variant counts (1, 2, 3), THEN per-model counts differ by more than 1 only where a model has no unchosen variant. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.StratifiedUnequalVariants`.
- **AC-CP-03 (CP-6, F-5)** GIVEN the allocator signature, THEN a `static_assert` (or signature test) proves `Players` is a set of opaque `ParticipantId` with no team, role, join-order or state member. GIVEN two rosters that differ **only in team assignment** and fixed entropy, WHEN the Round adapter (the code that turns Roster into `Players`) builds the call, THEN the resulting `AppearanceDeal` is byte-identical. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.TeamBlindMetamorphic`.
- **AC-CP-04 (F-8)** GIVEN `R = 2000` allocator calls on each suite seed, WHEN T1 to T7 run (T7 scaled by `(K - 1) / (K - r)`, T3b as a per-round permutation test), THEN no statistic rejects at `alpha = 0.001` and T3a is byte-identical. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.IndistinguishabilitySuite`.
- **AC-CP-04b (F-8, nightly)** GIVEN 100 other seed sets, WHEN the suite runs at `alpha = 0.001`, THEN at most 4 suite failures occur. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.HarnessCalibration` (nightly, not in the smoke set).
- **AC-CP-05 (F-8)** GIVEN allocators B1 (weighted dealing), B2 (the retired duplicates rule), B3 (teammates forbidden to share) and B4 (extras on the first appearances), WHEN the same suite runs, THEN each is rejected: B1 by T1, B2 by T1 and T5, B3 by T1, T5 and T6, B4 by T7. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.SuiteRejectsBiasedAllocators`. Passing AC-CP-04 while failing AC-CP-05 is a failure of the harness.
- **AC-CP-06 (CP-9)** GIVEN a fixed injected entropy value, WHEN the layout seed, round id, participant ids and server tick are each varied, THEN the deal is byte-identical (no input other than the entropy-derived root changes it); GIVEN two different entropy values with everything else fixed, THEN the deals differ; GIVEN the `Replace` and `Take` streams, THEN their outputs for a fixed root differ from the `Deal` stream's (domain separation, golden vectors). Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.SeedNotDerivable`. The absence of the injection hook in Shipping is checked by `CI.ShippingSymbolAudit`.
- **AC-CP-07 (CP-14)** GIVEN 2,000 independent round pairs for one participant with `K = 12` (independent entropy per pair), THEN the count of "same look as the previous round" lies in [135, 199] (99 percent binomial interval around 167; this AC uses 99 percent on purpose, it is a sanity check, not a leak gate); GIVEN a `Reset`, THEN the ledger is empty and a code-structure check proves the allocator is a free function with no static mutable state. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.NoCrossRoundMemory`.
- **AC-CP-08a (CP-6, Edge Cases)** GIVEN a second call in one round, THEN `Error_AlreadyDealt` and an unchanged ledger. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.ValidationLadder.SecondCall`.
- **AC-CP-08b (Edge Cases)** GIVEN `A = 14`, `D = 110`, THEN `K = 12` (the cap binds); GIVEN `A = 8`, `D = 50` (development), THEN `K = 8`. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.ValidationLadder.CapAndPoolBound`.
- **AC-CP-08c (CP-2)** GIVEN a pool below 6 models or 12 appearances in development, THEN `PoolBelowMvpFloor` is logged and the pool gate (AC-CP-22) fails. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.ValidationLadder.PoolFloor`. (Round's return to `Lobby` on `DeckInvalid` is an integration test owned by Round.)
- **AC-CP-09 (CP-8)** GIVEN a stub stream, THEN the rejection-sampled integer generator yields equal frequencies for a range of 3 over a 2-bit source (no modulo bias), and the shuffle output equals a reference Fisher-Yates driven by the same stub stream (golden test), with participants taking the first `|Players|` cards in canonical `ParticipantId` order; a code check proves no step after the deal sorts or groups cards by appearance (T4 is the statistical backstop). Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.ShuffleUnbiasedNoResort`.
- **AC-CP-28 (CP-6 call order)** GIVEN the allocator service, WHEN `AllocateAppearances` is called outside `Preparing`, before `OnTuningReady`, or after any pawn of the round has spawned, THEN it returns `Error_CallOrder` and changes nothing. Test `StealthGame.CharacterPool.Deck.CallOrderGuard`.
