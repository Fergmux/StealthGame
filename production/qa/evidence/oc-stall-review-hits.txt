round-match-framework.md:19 [timer,reveal] Round & Match Framework is the server-side system that turns ten connected people into a round and a round into exactly one result. It owns five things. (1) **Teams of up to five**: the size, balance and minimum-to-start rules; the Roster holds the data and Session takes the join requests. (2) **Five phases**, `Lobby`, `Preparing`, `Active`, `Ended` and `Reset`, with a stated precondition for every transition: `Preparing` waits for the frozen tuning (`OnTuningReady`), the layout (`OnLayoutReady(LayoutId, Seed)` from the Level Socket Contract; Round never depends on the Generator), the appearance deal, and every connected client's readiness, then places and spawns players mixed into the NPC crowd in one frame. (3) **The single resolution queue**: every command, objective completion and timer that can interact with another is stamped with one total-order key (server tick, then a monotonically increasing sequence id) and resolved serially, so two simultaneous events always have a first and a second. (4) **The win conditions**: `ObjectiveWin(team)` when the shared final objective completes and `EliminationWin(team)` when a team's Active count reaches zero, latched once, so a tie or a draw cannot exist; there is no match time limit and Round adds no timer or reveal that could end or steer a round. (5) **The consequences of disconnecting**: an absent player's pawn keeps walking as part of the crowd for a grace period, then the player becomes an informant, and the pawn joins the crowd for good. Round also keeps the **server-only Round Event Log** (the record of why every round ended, released to clients only in `Ended` and, in the MVP, only as a bare result), the round counter and match score, the host's start and reset commands, and the rule for what `Reset` clears.
round-match-framework.md:21 [clock] Players never see Round; they feel what it guarantees: a round that begins for all ten at the same instant, ends for all ten at the same instant with one winning team and a stated reason, never shows a clock or a head-count of rivals, and never penalises a dropped connection more than the loss of an active role. The win is decided in the **latch step**; everything observable about it happens together in one later server step, the **round-end step**. When the deciding item is a strike, the round-end step is that strike's death write (Assassination ASN-R21: `ContactServerTime − StrikeNetLeadSec`, 0.25 s after the slot at defaults, 0.60 s for a `BushStrike`), run in the same server step as the death write through Assassination's pending-write queue (ASN-R23; Assassination change request 14). from here Round's server gate treats the round as over (`TryTerminate` refuses, F4 is not evaluated, connection events change only the connection); (ii) the queue closes: every item still pending, and every command, AI item or timer item submitted before the round-end step, returns `Rejected_Latched` (logged), and the `NotNow` replies the IIF owes for them are held and sent at the round-end step, never before; round-match-framework.md:105 [clock] **R11. One queue, one total order.** There is exactly one resolution queue per round, on the server, open from `Active` entry to the latch (or abort). Every state-changing event that can interact with another is an **item** in it. The total-order key of an item is `Key = (ServerTickIndex, Seq)`: `ServerTickIndex` is a monotonically increasing integer incremented once per server game-thread frame at the queue's drain; `Seq` is a monotonically increasing 64-bit counter, assigned on the game thread when the item is **stamped** (R12: client items at drain start by the mailbox permutation, server items by the due-time rule: an item is **due** when `DueServerTime <= NowServerTime + DueEpsilonMs`, with `DueEpsilonMs` = 1 ms), restarting at 1 for each round. Keys are compared lexicographically; no two items share a key; the queue is drained in ascending `Seq`. **This is the tie-break key that Assassination and every other system must use.** Nobody may keep a feature-local clock or timestamp to order events against another system's events.
round-match-framework.md:106 [timer] - **Follow-ons resolve inline** (systems-designer).
